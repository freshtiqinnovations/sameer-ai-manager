Start with friction, not technology

The best first automation is usually where work repeats often, errors are costly, or a missed action loses a customer. Do not begin by asking “where can we use AI?” Ask “where does the same avoidable problem happen every week?”

Lead and customer checklist

Check whether enquiries are captured from every channel, whether someone owns each lead and whether follow-ups have a due time. If any answer is no, fix this before adding advanced AI.

  • Website forms enter one pipeline
  • WhatsApp enquiries are recorded
  • Source/UTM is stored
  • Owner assigned automatically
  • Overdue follow-ups are visible
  • Customer receives a clear human handoff

Operations checklist

Look for repeated copy-paste work, manual status calls, spreadsheet duplication, recurring reports and approvals that wait because nobody knows the next owner.

Safety checklist

Define actions that automation may perform by itself and actions that require approval. Money movement, account credentials, legal commitments and destructive actions should have explicit controls.

Choose the first project

Score each problem by frequency, time lost, customer impact and implementation difficulty. Start with something frequent, painful and measurable. A small proven workflow creates a better foundation than a large untested transformation.

Frequently asked questions

Should I automate everything at once?

No. Start with a measurable workflow, prove it, then expand.

How do I estimate ROI?

Estimate hours saved plus recovered leads, then compare that value with build and operating cost. Use our free ROI calculator for a first-pass estimate.

What if my process is not documented?

The automation audit should first map the current process and decision points before any coding starts.
