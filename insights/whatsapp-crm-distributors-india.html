When a WhatsApp group becomes an unreliable sales pipeline

A distributor might receive the same enquiry from a website form, a dealer's WhatsApp message and a salesperson's call. Without one lead record, two sales reps may quote different prices while nobody takes responsibility for the next action. An automation system should provide clear ownership and auditability—not just a chatbot reply.

For radiator, auto-parts, electrical, FMCG or building-materials distributors, useful workflow fields often include dealer name, location, product/SKU, requested quantity, urgency, assigned sales rep, quotation reference, promised follow-up time and status. The exact fields should match the company's products and permissions.

Example: from dealer message to approved quotation

  1. Customer-initiated enquiry: A dealer messages the business WhatsApp number with an item and quantity. An official WhatsApp Business Platform integration receives the message, subject to configured permissions.
  2. Minimum qualification: The assistant asks for missing product, quantity, delivery city and preferred callback details. Do not ask for card PINs or OTPs.
  3. Deduplication and routing: The CRM matches existing contacts and open enquiries, then assigns one responsible sales rep or territory owner.
  4. Stock and pricing lookup: The system checks an authorised live stock/price source. If unavailable, it marks the response pending staff confirmation; it must not invent availability or discounts.
  5. Quotation approval: The authorised person reviews price, taxes, freight and credit terms before an approved quote is shared. Payment links are generated only against valid commercial records.
  6. Next action: The CRM records delivery of the quotation, agreed callback time and status. A reminder appears for the assigned rep, with a handoff when the buyer wants a human.
  7. Order and collection tracking: Once an order is accepted, order status and collections are handled through appropriately permissioned systems. Customer reminders must follow consent and messaging rules.

Which parts should be AI, and which should be rules?

AI can interpret informal enquiries in Hindi, English or Hinglish, classify customer intent and draft replies. Deterministic rules should control credit limits, tax calculations, authorised pricing, account ownership, refund decisions and payment verification. A human should approve unusual requests, high-value changes and legal/commercial exceptions.

Illustrative example, not a claimed customer case study.

A dealer asks for 20 units of SKU R-204. If the inventory feed says “unknown”, a reliable bot answers that availability requires staff confirmation and creates a task. A risky bot guesses “available” and takes payment. The first workflow protects both the customer and distributor.

What an implementation project actually requires

  • Source of truth: Product catalogue and authorised current stock, ERP or approved spreadsheet.
  • Messaging: Official WhatsApp Business Platform access through Meta or a provider, sender configuration and customer permissions.
  • CRM: Sales roles, territory ownership, duplicate rules, reminders, reporting and an audit log.
  • Security: Encrypted integrations, restricted credentials, appropriate retention and least-privilege permissions.
  • Testing: Wrong SKU, duplicate lead, failed webhook, unknown price, no response, opt-out and payment-pending scenarios.
  • Handover: Documentation for staff, escalation owner, recurring platform charges and ongoing maintenance expectations.

How to measure value without invented ROI claims

Before rollout, record the current median first-response time, number of open enquiries without an owner, overdue follow-ups, number of quotes awaiting approval, and successful quote-to-order rate. Compare the same definitions after rollout, excluding demo/test leads and duplicate WhatsApp conversations. Results depend on team process and lead quality, not just the software.

Common questions

Can a distributor start without replacing its ERP?

Often yes. A small workflow can begin with website/WhatsApp enquiry capture and CRM follow-up, then add stock and quotation integrations only after permissions and data quality are clear.

Can the assistant quote customers automatically?

Only where current prices and commercial rules have been explicitly approved. Custom discounts, credits and unverified stock should go to an authorised employee.

Does WhatsApp allow automatic promotional follow-ups?

Business-initiated messages must follow the applicable WhatsApp Business Platform rules, including approved templates and customer opt-in requirements. Avoid unsolicited bulk messaging and use opt-out controls.

What does a first phase cost?

The cost depends on number of channels, users, integrations, approvals and support requirements. WhatsApp provider and messaging charges may be separate. Request a documented scope before payment; see published starting prices.
