What a production chatbot should do beyond answering FAQs

A useful chatbot should keep context across messages, ask only for missing information, recognise when a request needs a person and save the conversation in a form your team can act on. For sales, that often means business type, requirement, channel, urgency and preferred contact. For support, it means answering only from approved information and escalating account-specific or uncertain cases.

Recommended rollout

  1. Knowledge and guardrails: approved services, policies, FAQs and escalation rules.
  2. Conversation flow: greeting, enquiry understanding, qualification, booking or handoff.
  3. Integrations: CRM, spreadsheet, calendar, webhook or API only where reliable access exists.
  4. Review: test real questions, identify failure patterns and improve before scaling traffic.

What to measure

Useful metrics include first-response time, completed enquiry forms, qualified-lead rate, successful handoffs, booking requests and the percentage of conversations that needed correction. We do not promise a sales outcome; we make the workflow measurable so you can see whether it is helping.
