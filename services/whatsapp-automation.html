BEFORE YOU BUY

Setup, ownership, recurring costs and limitations

Setup requirements

We first confirm the business number/account setup, approved use case, message direction, languages, lead fields, human handoff rule and any CRM/calendar/API access needed.

Provider ownership

Production WhatsApp messaging should use official or supported business tools/providers. The written scope states which account/provider is customer-owned and who is responsible for provider approval.

Recurring costs

Meta/provider messaging fees, AI usage, hosting/VPS, phone-number/provider plans and optional managed support can be recurring third-party or support costs. They are separate unless explicitly included.

Integrations

CRM, ERP, Google Sheets, calendars, payment links, webhooks and APIs can be integrated when access and technical compatibility are verified during discovery.

Consent & templates

Customer-initiated and opted-in journeys are preferred. Business-initiated messaging may require approved templates and must follow applicable WhatsApp/provider rules.

Limitations

Automation cannot guarantee sales, provider approval, message delivery, a third-party API's uptime or a customer's response. Pricing, contracts and sensitive decisions can remain human-approved.

WhatsApp Business API rollout: decisions before launch

Use these checkpoints to decide what belongs in the initial project scope. Final fees, third-party subscriptions, delivery steps and approval roles belong in the written proposal.

  • Number and provider ownership: Confirm who owns the WhatsApp Business Account, sender number and provider relationship. Integration must be approved through official supported channels.
  • Lead qualification and handoff: Decide which enquiries the bot can handle, which details are saved in CRM and when a named employee must take over.
  • Template and messaging charges: Business-initiated messaging, template approval, opt-in requirements and provider charges should be explained separately from development fees.
  • Failure recovery: Test missed webhooks, duplicate messages, unknown pricing and customer opt-outs before enabling live customer follow-ups.

See the India distributor workflow example for quotation and stock-approval logic. You can also review starting prices and package scope.

WhatsApp chatbot workflows for clinics, retailers and service businesses

A business WhatsApp chatbot works best when it follows the real enquiry journey instead of sending the same automatic reply to everyone:

  • Clinics and appointment teams: Collect service, preferred date and callback details; confirm availability only after a calendar check or staff approval. See appointment booking automation.
  • Retailers and online stores: Route product questions, catalogue enquiries, approved prices and order-status requests to the correct team.
  • Real estate businesses: Capture budget, property type and location, then assign a human agent. Explore the Dubai real-estate workflow guide.
  • Distributors and field sales: Record dealer enquiries, assign ownership and send quotation requests through an integrated CRM.

Before deployment: Confirm official provider eligibility, opt-in requirements, staff handoff, data retention and recurring messaging or hosting costs. Try a live walkthrough on request or request a free workflow audit.
